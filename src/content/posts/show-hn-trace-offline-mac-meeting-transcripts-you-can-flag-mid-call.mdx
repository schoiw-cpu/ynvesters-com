You walk out of a one-hour Zoom call, open a blank doc, and realize you can barely remember who agreed to do what — let alone the exact wording of that critical decision made around the 40-minute mark. You either took scattered notes and missed half the conversation, or you were fully present and now have nothing written down. Both feel like losing.

That's the exact problem [Trace](https://traceapp.info) is built to solve. It's a native Mac app that transcribes your meetings entirely offline — no cloud upload, no third-party server ever seeing your audio — and crucially, it lets you tap a single key mid-call to flag moments worth revisiting. Think of it as a DVR bookmark button for your conversations, combined with a fully local transcript you own outright.

This guide will walk you through everything: what Trace actually does, how to get it running, how to use it during a live call, and how to get the most out of it after the meeting ends.

## What Is Trace, and Why Does "Offline" Matter?

Most meeting transcription tools — Otter.ai, Fireflies, Grain, and others — work by sending your audio to a cloud server where a speech model processes it and returns text. That works fine until you're discussing anything sensitive: a client's financials, a medical situation, a pre-announcement product roadmap, or simply a frank internal conversation you wouldn't want indexed somewhere.

Trace takes the opposite approach. It runs speech recognition entirely on your Mac using [Apple's on-device machine learning frameworks](https://developer.apple.com/machine-learning/), which means your audio never leaves your machine. There's no account required to start transcribing. No subscription sending data to a third party. The transcript lives in a local file on your computer, full stop.

This matters in practice for several reasons:

- **Privacy by architecture**: There's no breach risk from a vendor's server because your audio never hits one.
- **Works without internet**: Useful on planes, in spotty-signal conference rooms, or anywhere your Wi-Fi is unreliable.
- **No latency from round-trips**: Processing happens locally, so transcription starts fast.
- **No per-seat licensing tied to a cloud account**: Your data, your machine, your call.

For anyone operating under compliance requirements — HIPAA, SOC 2, legal privilege, or just corporate policy — this architectural difference isn't a minor feature, it's often the deciding factor.

## System Requirements Before You Install

Before you download, confirm your Mac meets Trace's requirements. Because local inference is computationally real, you need reasonably modern hardware:

- **macOS**: Ventura (13) or later is recommended. Sonoma and Sequoia work well.
- **Chip**: Apple Silicon (M1, M2, M3 series) is where you'll see the best performance. Intel Macs may run it but with higher CPU load.
- **RAM**: 8 GB is the minimum; 16 GB gives you headroom, especially if you're running the meeting app, Trace, and other tools simultaneously.
- **Storage**: The app itself is lightweight, but transcript files and any cached model weights will accumulate — budget a few hundred MB.

If you're unsure which chip your Mac has, go to  → **About This Mac**. The overview panel will show "Apple M1" (or M2/M3) for Silicon, or an Intel Core designation for older models.

## Step 1: Download and Install Trace

Head to [traceapp.info](https://traceapp.info) and download the latest release. At the time of writing, Trace is distributed directly from the developer's site rather than the Mac App Store, so you'll be opening a `.dmg` file.

**Installation steps:**

1. Open the downloaded `.dmg` file.
2. Drag the Trace icon into your Applications folder.
3. Launch Trace from Applications or Spotlight (`Cmd + Space`, type "Trace").
4. On first launch, macOS will ask you to confirm you want to open an app from an identified developer. Click **Open**.
5. Trace will prompt you for **Microphone access** — grant it. Without this permission, it cannot capture audio from your calls.

That's it. No account creation, no email, no credit card. You're in.

## Step 2: Understand the Interface Before Your First Call

Trace's interface is intentionally minimal. When you open it, you'll see:

- **A large record/stop button** — this starts and stops a transcription session.
- **A flag button (or keyboard shortcut)** — this drops a timestamped marker into the live transcript.
- **A transcript pane** — live text appears here as you speak or as meeting participants speak.
- **Session history** — past transcripts are listed by date/time so you can find any previous meeting.

Take two minutes to locate the flag shortcut before your first real meeting. You don't want to be hunting for it while someone is delivering important context. The flagging feature is the key differentiator here — it's what separates Trace from simply leaving a voice recorder running.

## Step 3: Running Trace During a Live Meeting

Here's a typical workflow for a 9 AM Zoom standup:

1. **Open your meeting app** (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, whatever you use) and join the call as normal.
2. **Open Trace and hit record** a few seconds before or right as the meeting starts. The transcript pane will begin filling with text as audio plays through your system or microphone.
3. **Participate normally**. You don't need to narrate anything special for Trace. Just be in the meeting.
4. **When something important happens** — a decision, an action item, a number someone quotes, a deadline — **press the flag shortcut**. Trace drops a colored marker at that exact timestamp in the transcript.
5. **At the end of the meeting, hit stop**. The session saves automatically.

The flag feature is worth emphasizing. In a 60-minute meeting, you might flag four or five moments. When you review the transcript later, those flags let you jump straight to the 12-minute mark where the budget was approved and the 47-minute mark where someone committed to a deadline — without scrolling through the full text.

## Step 4: Reviewing and Using Your Transcript After the Call

After the meeting, open the saved session from Trace's history. You'll see the full transcript with your flags highlighted inline. From here you can:

- **Jump between flags** to review only the moments you marked as important.
- **Search the transcript** for specific names, numbers, or terms.
- **Copy text** to paste into a follow-up email, a Notion doc, a Jira ticket, or wherever you track action items.
- **Export** the transcript as a text or structured file, depending on the version you're running.

One practical habit: immediately after a meeting, spend three minutes scanning your flagged moments and paste the key decisions and action items into your team's project management tool. The transcript gives you the verbatim wording, which matters when there's ambiguity about what was actually agreed.

## Practical Tips for Better Transcripts

Getting clean transcripts is partly about the tool and partly about your setup:

- **Use a dedicated microphone or AirPods** rather than your MacBook's built-in mic. External mics reduce background noise significantly and improve word accuracy.
- **Ask participants to introduce themselves** at the start if you're in a large multi-speaker call. Trace captures who's speaking based on audio, and a brief intro helps you mentally map voices to names when reviewing.
- **Flag liberally, not sparingly**. It costs nothing to drop an extra marker, and the two seconds it takes is worth it when you're searching for context an hour later.
- **Close unnecessary audio apps** before recording. Other apps fighting for audio input can cause gaps or artifacts.
- **Check your macOS audio routing** in System Settings → Sound if Trace isn't picking up the meeting audio. You may need to point it at the correct input source, especially with virtual meeting tools. Tools like [BlackHole](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole) (a free open-source virtual audio driver) can help route system audio into Trace if your setup requires it.
